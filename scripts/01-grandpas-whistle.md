# 01: Grandpa's Whistle

**Title:** I Told Grandpa To Stop Whistling 😔
**Alt titles (A/B):** "He Never Stopped Whistling 🎶" · "Two Notes Saved My Life 🏔️"
**Target length:** ~80s (≈215 words at the competitor's ~160 wpm)
**Narrator:** first person, the grandson, telling it as an adult (same calm low voice as the reference)

---

## VOICEOVER (paste this exactly into ElevenLabs)

Every time I left Grandpa's house, he'd stand on the porch and whistle two notes. High, then low.
Since I was six, I'd whistle them right back.
He said it meant, "Come home safe."
But when I turned fifteen, my friends heard it, and they laughed so hard they whistled it at me for a week.
So the next time he whistled, I didn't whistle back.
I turned around and said, "Grandpa, I'm not a dog. Stop doing that."
He just nodded.
And I never heard him whistle again.
Two winters later, I went snowboarding with those same friends and cut off the trail to show off.
The fog rolled in so thick I couldn't see my own board.
My phone died. It got dark.
I screamed until my voice gave out.
Search teams looked for six hours and found nothing.
I was curled up under a pine tree, shaking, when I heard it.
High, then low.
I hadn't heard it in two years, but my lips whistled back before my brain did.
High, then low. Again. Closer.
Then a flashlight hit my face.
Grandpa. Seventy-four years old, waist-deep in snow.
The rescue team had told him to wait at the lodge. He didn't.
On the ride down, I asked him how he knew I'd answer.
He said, "Kiddo, I've whistled every single time you left my house.
Just quiet enough that you wouldn't hear."

## Pronunciation / delivery notes
- "Seventy-four" is written out on purpose (say it, don't let TTS read "74").
- "High, then low." Each time, slow down a hair; it is the motif. Do not whistle in the VO; I add a real two-note whistle SFX in the edit.
- "He just nodded." is said flat and quiet, the guilt beat.
- The last line is softer and slower than everything else. No music under the final 2 words.
- Keep the energy calm and intimate. The reference narrator never shouts; even "I screamed" is said, not screamed.
